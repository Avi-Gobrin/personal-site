Abstract

We present a comprehensive study of the author, a specimen of the genus mathematicus appliedus, currently observed in the wild at the University of Toronto. Through a combination of theoretical results, computational experiments, and questionable life choices, we establish the existence of a unique career trajectory $\gamma^*$ in the space of quantitative finance roles satisfying certain optimality conditions. Along the way, we construct a chess engine, prove several theorems of dubious practical value, and reach Grandmaster in StarCraft II. We conjecture that rigorous thinking actually matters somewhere.

Keywords: applied mathematics, statistics, quantitative finance, chess engines, existential optimization, competitive gaming

1. Introduction

The problem of finding an optimal career trajectory has attracted significant attention in the literature, particularly among graduating students with bills to pay. Let $\Omega$ denote the space of all possible career outcomes, equipped with the natural $\sigma$-algebra of "things that could go wrong." We seek a path $\gamma: [0,T] \to \Omega$ maximizing the functional

$$J[\gamma] = \int_0^T \Big( u\big(\gamma(t)\big) - c\big(\gamma'(t)\big) \Big)\, dt$$

where $u$ denotes the utility derived from meaningful work and $c$ represents the cost of getting there, including but not limited to: analysis homework, measure theory, and debugging segmentation faults at 2 AM.1

The author is an applied mathematics specialist at the University of Toronto, with a major in statistics and a minor in computer science. Most of his time goes toward hard problems, whether that means analysis proofs, building things from scratch, or figuring out why his chess engine keeps hanging its queen.2

Outside of academics, the author reached Grandmaster in StarCraft II (Zerg), plays competitive Magic: The Gathering, and reads more than is probably reasonable. He is currently focused on quantitative finance and looking for roles where rigorous thinking actually matters.

2. Preliminary Results

Definition 2.1 (Competence). We say an individual $x$ is competent in domain $D$ if there exists a measure $\mu$ on the space of problems $\mathcal{P}_D$ such that $\Pr[\text{$x$ solves $p$}] > 0.5$ for $\mu$-almost every $p \in \mathcal{P}_D$.

Theorem 2.2 (Employment Existence). The author is currently seeking employment. Moreover, this state is non-trivially persistent.

Proof. Immediate from the observation that the author is writing a personal website disguised as a mathematics paper. The persistence follows from the boundary condition that rent is due monthly.

Lemma 2.3. Let $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be the author's utility function over career attributes (intellectual challenge, compensation, coworker quality, free coffee). Then $f$ is strictly concave in each argument, and the unique maximizer $x^*$ satisfies

$$x^* = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{F}} f(x) \quad \text{where } \mathcal{F} = \{\text{quantitative finance roles}\}$$

3. Main Results (Projects)

In this section, we present the author's principal contributions, each framed as a constructive proof of competence in a relevant domain.

Theorem 3.1 (The Chess Engine). There exists a chess engine, implemented in Python, employing alpha-beta pruning with iterative deepening and hand-tuned evaluation functions, such that:

(i) it evaluates positions with non-trivial accuracy,
(ii) it occasionally plays good moves,
(iii) it hangs its queen with probability bounded above by $\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon$ is regrettably nonzero.

Proof. Constructive. The engine exists; the author wrote it. Correctness of the alpha-beta search follows from the minimax theorem (von Neumann, 1928). The queen-hanging behavior remains an open problem.

Proposition 3.2 (Monte Carlo Option Pricer). For a sufficiently large class of exotic derivatives, the author has constructed a pricer achieving variance reduction through antithetic variates and control variates, satisfying

$$\text{Var}[\hat{V}_n] = O(n^{-1-\delta}) \quad \text{for some } \delta > 0$$

Because closed-form solutions are for quitters.

Proposition 3.3 (Statistical Learning Toolkit). From-scratch implementations of core machine learning algorithms exist in the author's repository, including: linear/logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, $k$-NN, PCA, and gradient descent variants. No sklearn was imported in the making of these implementations.3

Corollary 3.4 (Competitive Programming Archive). The author maintains an archive of solutions and write-ups from competitive programming contests. These serve primarily as lessons in humility.

6. Conclusion and Future Work

We have established the existence of a career trajectory $\gamma^*$ satisfying the necessary optimality conditions. However, the uniqueness of $\gamma^*$ remains open, as does the question of whether the author will converge to it in finite time. Future work includes:

(i) securing a quantitative finance role where rigorous thinking is valued,

(ii) teaching the chess engine to stop hanging its queen,

(iii) proving Axiom (A3) from first principles,

(iv) cooking something that is not pasta.
