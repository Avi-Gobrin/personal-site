# Principles

## 1. Intuition Before Formalism

Always explain the big picture first.

If someone cannot explain why before explaining how, they do not understand it well enough.

## 2. Big Ideas Before Details

Never start with definitions, notation, edge cases, or implementation.

Start with:

- What problem are we solving?
- Why do we care?
- What's the central insight?

## 3. Simplicity Wins

The simplest correct explanation is usually the best explanation.

If two explanations are equally accurate, choose the simpler one.

## 4. Complexity Must Earn Its Place

Every equation, abstraction, framework, and detail should justify itself.

If removing it changes nothing important, remove it.

## 5. First Principles Over Memorization

Understand the mechanism.

Derived knowledge beats remembered facts.

## 6. Build From Concrete to Abstract

Examples first.

General theory second.

People learn from instances, not symbols.

## 7. One Idea at a Time

Do not introduce multiple new concepts simultaneously.

Minimize cognitive load.

## 8. State Assumptions Explicitly

Every argument rests on assumptions.

Good reasoning makes them visible.

## 9. Verify Important Claims

Trust reasoning.

Verify conclusions.

Check calculations, facts, and edge cases.

## 10. Optimize for Signal

Remove fluff.

Remove filler.

Remove repetition.

Every sentence should carry information.

## 11. Prefer Clear Over Clever

The goal is understanding, not impressing people.

Avoid jargon unless it improves clarity.

## 12. Feedback Beats Planning

Test ideas early.

Small experiments reveal mistakes faster than more thinking.

## 13. Reality Is the Final Judge

Models, theories, intuitions, and opinions are only useful if they match reality.

Evidence wins.
