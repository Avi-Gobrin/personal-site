Engagement, Performance, and Student Outcomes at the Open University

Apr 2026 · Course project · STA437, Methods for Multivariate Data

Over 23,301 student registrations, logistic regression lifts Distinction recall from 3.2% to 39.3%.

The Open University Learning Analytics Dataset records six behavioral features for 25,820 student-course registrations, 23,301 of them complete, each labeled Distinction, Fail, Pass, or Withdrawn. This report runs a full multivariate pipeline (EDA, Mahalanobis-distance normality testing, PCA, varimax factor analysis, Gaussian mixture modeling, and LDA) to ask whether unsupervised structure in assessment behavior recovers the known outcomes, and whether features observable before any grade is issued (submission count and timing) are enough to flag withdrawal early. Each method is motivated by the result before it.

Findings

  • Parallel analysis and varimax rotation give three factors: Engagement (submission count and timing; top communality h² = 0.84), Consistent Performance (mean score and score SD; top communality h² = 0.72), and Prior Commitment (credits and prior attempts)
  • FA, GMM, and LDA converge on the same two dimensions; Prior Commitment describes academic history but barely moves LDA or the clusters
  • GMM at K = 4 reaches an adjusted Rand index of 0.137: assessment behavior partly recovers the four outcomes, and Distinction and Pass students share the high-engagement cluster
  • Under 5-fold CV, LDA reaches 64.5% against a 53.0% baseline with 63.2% Withdrawn recall but 3.2% Distinction recall; logistic regression reaches 67.0% and lifts Distinction recall to 39.3% by dropping the shared-covariance assumption

Methods

PCA · factor analysis · Gaussian mixture models · LDA · logistic regression
